# Human review of fictional recommendations

Automated technical acceptance does not measure subjective relevance. No human ratings have been recorded yet.

Use the three fictional sample profiles. Review each recommendation and the rationale before and after structured feedback. Do not use real child information or treat these unreviewed activities as real-world guidance.

For each recommendation, record **Yes / No / Unsure**:

- Does it fit the fictional age, reported abilities, chosen focus and available materials?
- Is it feasible within the selected duration and preferred way to play?
- Does the explanation accurately reflect the profile and only feedback that was actually recorded?
- After “enjoyed + struggled,” does the next choice offer gentler same-skill practice without an unsupported jump? After “not interested,” does the format change?
- Are instructions, supervision, prototype limitations and the actual live/mock/fallback label clear?

Flag any invented facts, inappropriate activity, confusing rationale, pressure/comparison, or safety concern. Stop that demonstration path and record the issue rather than rating it relevant.

Suggested review record: date, reviewer initials/role, fictional profile ID, activity ID, recommendation mode, prompt/rules/library versions, the five answers, overall relevance (Yes/No/Unsure), and a brief non-sensitive issue note. Do not capture keys, access codes, request headers or environment files.

Only after a person completes this review should human relevance be reported: **overall Yes ratings / all reviewed recommendations**; keep No and Unsure counts visible. Report sample size and selection method. Do not infer clinical validity or population-wide relevance from a small classroom review. Do not change the current “Not measured” dashboard label until actual records exist.
